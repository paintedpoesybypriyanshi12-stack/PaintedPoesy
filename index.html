About Me

A little structure. A little surrender.

I’m Priyanshi Pandey, a Dallas-based mixed-media artist exploring the space between inherited stories and personal expression.

My professional life is in identity security—a world that asks for precision, clear boundaries, and carefully considered decisions. Painting gives me a different kind of space: one where I can begin with intention without needing to know exactly how everything will end.

What starts as a release from the demands of the day becomes a conversation with color, movement, and material.

My way of seeing

I am drawn to the moment when a familiar idea becomes unfamiliar enough to see again.

Hindu scriptures, traditions, and symbolism offer starting points for that exploration. I approach them with curiosity and respect, asking what their imagery and ideas might mean within a contemporary life—and how I might express that meaning without simply illustrating it.

A gemstone becomes more than a color to match. It becomes a question to explore.

What might quiet strength look like? Can a painting feel luminous without feeling delicate? How do you give uncertainty a form without resolving it?

My paintings are personal responses to those questions. They are not literal depictions of scripture or instructions on what to believe. They are a way of bringing something inherited into a language I can make my own.

Between flow and intention

I work with fluid acrylics and layered embellishments, allowing movement and deliberate placement to share the same surface.

The poured passages introduce something I cannot entirely predict. Then I slow down: looking, responding, and building texture where the painting seems to ask for it. Some areas remain open. Others gather into dense, light-catching forms.

I am interested in that contrast—the part that happens freely and the part that asks for patience.

Neither makes sense without the other.

Why I paint

Painting helps me quiet the noise without asking every thought to disappear.

It gives my attention somewhere tangible to rest: a current of color, the edge of a shape, the small decision of what to place next. There is relief in making something that does not need to solve a problem to have value.

I hope the finished work offers a little of that space to someone else. Not necessarily the same story I began with, but an invitation to pause, look closely, and discover a meaning of their own.

I begin with a story. The painting is where I learn what it means to me.

Materials and Process

Where the pour ends, another kind of attention begins.

My process moves between two rhythms: allowing and arranging.

Fluid acrylics establish the first movement. I respond to the paths they create, then build selected areas with carefully placed embellishments. The surface develops through that exchange—not entirely planned, and not entirely left to chance.

  1. Begin with meaning

    A work often starts with a gemstone and an idea I want to explore through it.

    I consider its traditional place within Navarathna, then ask what feels personally meaningful. That question helps me find an emotional direction before I settle on a visual one.

    The reference gives me somewhere to begin, not a fixed image to reproduce.

  2. Let color move

    The pour introduces its own possibilities.

    Colors meet, separate, reveal, and conceal. I work with that movement rather than expecting every passage to follow an exact plan. Sometimes the most compelling part is the one I would not have thought to design.

    This stage asks me to respond rather than insist.

  3. Build the surface

    The next stage asks for a slower kind of looking.

    I place embellishments in response to what has already emerged—gathering them in some areas, leaving room in others. Texture becomes a second language, sometimes following the painted movement and sometimes interrupting it.

    What began as a fluid gesture becomes a series of deliberate, individual decisions.

  4. Leave room for the viewer

    I do not want to close every interpretation.

    The title and symbolism offer one way into the painting. Your own memories, associations, and responses may offer another. Both can belong.

The meaning begins before the painting. It does not end with me.

Current Series

Navarathna

Nine gems. An evolving language of color.

In Navarathna, I take the idea of a jewel beyond its setting.

Inspired by the nine-gem tradition of Indian astrology, this series translates gemstone symbolism into expansive fields of poured color, reflective detail, and raised texture. Each work begins with a traditional association, then moves toward something personal: a feeling, a tension, a question I want to spend time with.

I am not trying to paint what a gemstone looks like.

I am exploring what it might feel like to step inside the idea of one.

  • Nine gemstone inspirations
  • Mixed media on canvas
  • Each artwork measures 5 × 4 ft

The tradition behind the series

Navaratna means “nine gems.” The traditional grouping brings together ruby, pearl, red coral, emerald, yellow sapphire, diamond, blue sapphire, hessonite, and cat’s-eye.

Each corresponds to one of the Navagraha of Indian astrology: the Sun, Moon, five visible planets, and the two lunar nodes, Rahu and Ketu. These are traditional astrological associations rather than a modern astronomical classification.

This tradition gives the series its starting structure. My interpretation begins where that structure meets lived experience.

I consider how an inherited symbol might speak to the present—not only through its established associations, but through the colors, contrasts, and emotional possibilities it opens up for me.

Tradition, translated

For me, working with tradition does not mean holding it at a distance or repeating its forms unchanged.

It means entering into conversation with it.

In these paintings, I move away from recognizable gemstone shapes and literal celestial imagery. Meaning emerges instead through movement, density, interruption, and light. A quiet passage may sit beside an intensely textured one. A flowing line may disappear beneath a cluster, then return somewhere unexpected.

The result is contemporary abstraction with a cultural point of departure: rooted in a tradition, but open to a personal reading.

You do not need to know the symbolism to enter the work. Color can be the first connection. The story can unfold afterward.

Explore the works

Each painting measures 5 × 4 ft, creating room for both expansive movement and intimate detail.

Seen from a distance, the composition offers a larger rhythm. Up close, its smaller decisions come into view: a delicate vein of color, a shift in texture, a gathering of reflective fragments.

The traditional associations introduce each work. The reflections that follow are my own.

A tradition can be a beginning, not a boundary.

Navarathna is my way of staying in conversation with inherited ideas while allowing my own visual language to develop.

The series brings together qualities I often experience as opposites: structure and instinct, softness and strength, stillness and movement. Painting gives them a place to exist together without needing to cancel one another out.

I hope you find something here that stays with you—a color, a detail, a question, or simply a reason to look a little longer.

About My Practice

Navarathna is a contemporary artistic interpretation of the Indian nine-gem tradition. References to celestial associations describe traditional beliefs; the paintings are not presented as astrological remedies or therapeutic objects. Gemstone names identify each work’s inspiration, not a claim that it contains those gemstones.

Navarathna is an ongoing series. New works will be added as the collection develops.
